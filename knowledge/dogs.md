# Dog care

General guidance for planning a dog's day. Not veterinary advice: an owner's vet
always overrides these notes.

## Walks and exercise
tags: walk, exercise, energy, puppy, senior, breed
Most adult dogs need at least two walks a day. Plan a longer walk (30-60 minutes)
for high-energy or working breeds such as huskies, border collies, shepherds and
retrievers, and shorter walks (15-30 minutes) for small, senior or low-energy dogs.
Puppies do better with several short outings than one long one. Morning and evening
walks are the most common pattern because they bracket the owner's working day.
Schedule walks as high priority: a skipped walk affects the dog's behaviour and health.

## Feeding
tags: feeding, food, meals, puppy, adult, senior
Adult dogs are usually fed twice a day, about 8-12 hours apart (for example morning
and evening). Puppies under six months usually eat three or four smaller meals a day.
Keep meal times consistent from day to day. A meal takes about 10 minutes including
setup and clean-up. Avoid strenuous exercise for roughly an hour after a large meal,
especially in large, deep-chested breeds.

## Bathroom breaks
tags: potty, bathroom, puppy, toilet, alone
Adult dogs should get a chance to relieve themselves at least every 6-8 hours.
Puppies need a bathroom break roughly every 2-3 hours while awake and after meals,
play and naps. A quick break takes about 5-10 minutes.

## Enrichment and training
tags: enrichment, training, play, puzzle, boredom, mental
Daily mental exercise helps prevent boredom: 10-15 minutes of training, a puzzle
feeder or a sniffing game. Short sessions work better than long ones, especially
for puppies. Enrichment is usually medium or low priority and can fill gaps in the day.

## Grooming
tags: grooming, brushing, coat, nails, teeth, dental
Long-haired and double-coated breeds benefit from brushing several times a week
(10-15 minutes). Short-coated dogs need brushing about weekly. Brushing teeth daily
or several times a week supports dental health. Nail trims are typically every 3-4
weeks. Grooming is usually low priority unless the vet has said otherwise.
