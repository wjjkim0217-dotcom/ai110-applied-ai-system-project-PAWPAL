# Planning a pet-care day

How to turn pet needs into a schedule that a busy owner can follow.

## Priorities
tags: priority, high, medium, low, important
Use high priority for anything that affects health or safety: medication, meals,
walks and bathroom breaks. Use medium priority for routine care such as litter box
cleaning and play. Use low priority for nice-to-have tasks such as grooming and extra
enrichment, which can move or be skipped on a busy day.

## Fixed times versus flexible tasks
tags: fixed time, flexible, routine, consistent
Give a task a fixed time only when the time matters: medication, meals the pet expects
at a set time, or an appointment. Leave other tasks flexible so the scheduler can fit
them into free gaps. Too many fixed-time tasks make conflicts more likely.

## Several pets, one owner
tags: multiple pets, two pets, conflict, overlap
The owner can only do one task at a time, so tasks for different pets at the same
time will conflict. Stagger fixed-time tasks by at least the length of the earlier
task, for example feed the cat at 18:00 and the dog at 18:10.

## Fitting the owner's day
tags: work, availability, busy, window, morning, evening
Tasks must fit inside the owner's available hours. If the owner works during the day,
put walks and meals before and after work and leave flexible tasks to fill the gaps.
If not everything fits, keep high-priority tasks and tell the owner which lower
priority tasks were left out.
